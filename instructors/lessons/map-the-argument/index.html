03 · What's the Argument?

See how an argument fits together

Identify the reasons, conclusion, and background, then show what supports what.

20 min suggested5 min preparationIntroductorySecondary and adult learners

What students will learn

  • Distinguish the conclusion from its supporting reasons and background.
  • Show the direction of support using an arrow or a written explanation.
  • Explain why a true detail need not be a reason for this conclusion.

Prerequisites: None

Format: Individual writing, pairs, and class discussion

The scenarios are invented for reasoning practice. Suggested timings can be adjusted to your class.

Materials

  • One student handout per learner, or display the activities and use paper.
  • Pens or pencils; no student devices required.
  • Keep the instructor guide and model answers separate from student materials.

Before class

  • Read the three scenarios and their model answers.
  • Print the student handout or display one activity at a time.
  • Explain that all scenarios are invented and students should use only the supplied facts.

Useful words

  • Conclusion: The statement the argument gives reasons to accept.
  • Reason: A statement offered in support of the conclusion.
  • Background: Context that helps describe the situation without being offered as support.

Your teaching plan

  1. 4 min
    Find two supporting reasons

    Read the gallery warm-up. Learners mark the two reasons and the conclusion, then compare arrows with a partner.

  2. 7 min
    Map the fundraising argument

    Learners label the main activity and draw its support arrow. Ask why the meeting day is background.

  3. 4 min
    Discuss sentence roles

    Reveal the model answer. Use the prompts to distinguish a statement's position from its role.

  4. 5 min
    Transfer the skill

    Learners complete the picnic exit ticket independently. Check their labels, arrow, and explanation before sharing the answer.

01 / Warm-up

The closed gallery

Invented scenario: A visitor says, 'The gallery is closed every Monday. Today is Monday. Therefore, the gallery is closed today.'

Your task: Write the conclusion and the two reasons. Show how the reasons support the conclusion.

  1. Copy the statement being supported.
  2. Copy both supporting statements.
  3. Draw arrows from the two reasons to the conclusion, or write how the reasons work together.

Before you finish: Do both reasons lead toward the statement about today, rather than away from it?

Model answer & explanation

Conclusion: the gallery is closed today. Reasons: it is closed every Monday, and today is Monday. The two reasons work together: today belongs to the set of days when the gallery is closed.

Why this works: It names all three roles and shows how the rule and the fact about today jointly support the conclusion.

02 / Main activity

Compare two fundraisers

Invented scenario: A club member writes: 'Our club meets on Tuesdays. Last month's bake sale earned $300 after costs, while the car wash earned $180. Therefore, we should hold another bake sale next month.'

Your task: Label the three sentences, draw one support arrow, and write one explanatory sentence.

  1. Label each sentence background, reason, or conclusion.
  2. Draw an arrow from the reason to the conclusion, or write 'reason -> conclusion' using the actual sentences.
  3. Explain in one sentence why the Tuesday meeting detail is not support for the fundraising recommendation.

Before you finish: Does your arrow show which statement supports which? The most detailed sentence need not be the conclusion.

Model answer & explanation

Tuesday meetings are background. The $300 versus $180 earnings comparison is the reason. Holding another bake sale is the conclusion. Draw the arrow from the earnings comparison to the recommendation. Meeting on Tuesday supplies no comparison of the two fundraising options.

Why this works: The answer maps the argument's structure and explains why a true detail is not necessarily a reason.

Guide the discussion

  • Must the conclusion be the last sentence?No. An argument can begin with its conclusion. Identify which statement the others support, rather than relying on sentence order.
  • Does finding a reason show that it is a good reason?No. Mapping identifies its intended role. Evaluating its accuracy, relevance, or strength is a further task.

Common confusions

  • The most detailed sentence is the conclusion.Detail does not decide the role. In the fundraiser, the detailed earnings comparison supports the recommendation.
  • A true background fact must support the conclusion.Truth and a supporting connection are different. The meeting day can be true without favoring either fundraiser.
  • Every argument has exactly one reason.The gallery example needs two reasons working together. Other arguments can have more.

03 / Check understanding

The picnic plan

Invented scenario: A club member writes, 'Our club was founded in 2018. The forecast predicts heavy rain during tomorrow's picnic. We should move tomorrow's picnic indoors.'

Your task: Label the three sentences and show the support relation; explain one label.

  1. Label each sentence background, reason, or conclusion.
  2. Draw an arrow from the supporting sentence to the conclusion, or write the relation in words.
  3. Explain why the founding year does not support the proposed move.

Before you finish: Have you identified what supports the recommendation without treating the weather forecast as a guarantee?

Model answer & explanation

Background: the club was founded in 2018. Reason: heavy rain is forecast during the picnic. Conclusion: move tomorrow's picnic indoors. The forecast supports the proposed move; the founding year says nothing about tomorrow's weather or location.

Why this works: The answer maps the argument without claiming that the forecast guarantees rain or that no other considerations matter.

Look for these signs of understanding

  • The conclusion and reason are correctly identified.
  • The arrow or explanation shows support flowing from reason to conclusion.
  • Background is distinguished by its lack of a supporting connection to this recommendation.

Adapt to your class

  • More supportSupply the three role labels and let learners arrange them beside each sentence. Ask them to complete: "This reason supports this conclusion because..."
  • More challengeAsk learners to put the fundraiser conclusion first while preserving its reason. Expected answer: "We should hold another bake sale because it earned $300 after costs, compared with $180 for the car wash." The order changes; the support relation does not.
