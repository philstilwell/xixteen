Student activity · 10 How Likely Is It?

Make sense of a chance forecast

Read a probability as a claim about a defined event, then judge a record rather than one outcome.

All scenarios are invented for reasoning practice. Work through each task using only the supplied facts. You can write on paper or use the response boxes. Responses are not saved or submitted.

Download handout · PDF

Words to use

  • Probability: A stated chance that a defined event will occur.
  • Calibration: A match between stated chances and outcome frequencies across many comparable forecasts.
  • Comparable forecasts: Forecasts made for similar events, conditions, and time horizons.

01 / Warm-up

A chance of arriving late

Invented scenario: A travel app gives tomorrow's bus a 25% chance of reaching the terminal after 9 a.m. Imagine 100 comparable forecasts made one day ahead at 25% by a well-calibrated app: its chances match long-run frequencies.

Your task: Write two approximate counts and one sentence about the chance of tomorrow being late.

  1. State about how many of the 100 buses should arrive after 9 a.m. and how many should not.
  2. Explain why a 25% forecast does not rule out a late bus tomorrow.

Before you finish: Keep the event “after 9 a.m.” clear; do not turn “unlikely” into “impossible”.

02 / Main activity

What does 70% rain mean?

Invented scenario: A weather app assigns a 70% chance of measurable rain tomorrow. Here, measurable rain means at least 1 mm at the town weather station during that calendar day. Imagine 100 comparable forecasts, each issued one day ahead at 70%, from a well-calibrated app: its stated chances match long-run frequencies.

Your task: Write two approximate counts, an explanation of one dry day, and a plan for checking the forecast record.

  1. Write approximately how many of those 100 days should have measurable rain and how many should not.
  2. Explain why a dry tomorrow would not by itself refute a 70% forecast.
  3. Describe the record you would inspect to judge whether the app's 70% forecasts are well calibrated.

Before you finish: Do not treat 70% as a guarantee, 70% of the town, or 70% of the day.

03 / Check your understanding

A delivery forecast record

Invented scenario: A courier predicts an 80% chance that a parcel will arrive by 6 p.m. tomorrow. Its record contains 200 comparable one-day-ahead forecasts made at 80%; 160 parcels arrived by 6 p.m. and 40 did not.

Your task: Write a frequency calculation and two sentences judging the record and a late parcel.

  1. Calculate the on-time percentage: on-time parcels / all parcels x 100.
  2. State whether this record matches the stated 80% chance, without claiming it guarantees future accuracy.
  3. Explain why one late parcel would not by itself show an 80% forecast was wrong.

Before you finish: Include all 200 forecasts and preserve the possibility of a late parcel.

Before you finish

  • The forecast is tied to its stated event, threshold, and time period.
  • The frequency calculation includes all relevant cases and is correct.
  • The interpretation preserves uncertainty and distinguishes a record from a single result.
